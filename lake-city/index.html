How Lake City Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

Most early failures start with maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Flush the tank every year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and operate the relief valve each year so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees for safer water and less scale. If you have a tankless unit, descale it yearly where the water is hard to protect the heat exchanger.

The age of your heater plays a big role. With regular maintenance, a standard tank typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like rust-colored water, popping noises, or moisture under the tank, it's time to plan a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and is far less stressful than an emergency swap after a leak.

The earlier you report a hot water problem, the cheaper it usually is. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble needs only a flush, yet that same buildup, ignored for a few years, can overheat the bottom of the tank and shorten the life of the tank, forcing a replacement much sooner. Save our number in your contacts today so help is one call away the moment something seems off. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.
